This is probably the most carefully crafted deck on the internet for Computer Architecture, Boolean logic, and RISC-V assembly. It is based on the course "Computer Architecture 1" taught in the first year of Italian universities of Computer Science. It's not just a simple set of flashcards to “review on the fly”: some cards are quite long, but this choice ensures comprehensive explanations without overly fragmenting the material.

This deck is designed both for those who want to learn the fundamentals of information theory, Boolean logic, and Finite State Machines (FSM), and for those who wish to learn RISC-V assembly or understand in detail how a processor works. It is not an exhaustive guide, but a complete support that makes it easier to grasp the theoretical concepts.

I have structured each flashcard clearly and formatted it well, including all essential information and various illustrations for visual understanding. Separate subdecks also include solved exercises, reference tables, CPU simulators (and other embeds of small interactive circuits), and much more (not meant to be memorized, but very useful to truly understand the theory).

### Deck structure:

```
Computer Architecture
├── 01 Computer, Performance, and Logic Circuits
│   ├── 01 Computer and Performance
│   ├── 02 Information Representation
│   └── 03 Logic Circuits
├── 02 ISA RISC-V & Procedure Calling
│   ├── Assembly Exercises
│   └── Instruction Reference
└── 03 Processor (Datapath, Control, and Pipelining)
    ├── Exercises

```

🖼️ There are many SVG vector images included (to keep the deck lightweight and guarantee pixel-perfect quality) and custom CSS styles. It is recommended to download the entire deck to preview the cards correctly. I have ensured compatibility with both Anki's light and dark themes, and to some extent with mobile devices as well.

📕 Part of the material is based on the book *Computer Organization and Design (RISC-V Edition): The Hardware Software Interface* by Patterson & Hennessy, but:

* Many chapters have been condensed or simplified to avoid superfluous details at the beginning of the deck.
* Introductory sections on Boolean algebra, combinational/sequential logic, information encoding, and other topics have been added to make the study more accessible for beginners.
* Some overly detailed or non-essential parts have been excluded.
